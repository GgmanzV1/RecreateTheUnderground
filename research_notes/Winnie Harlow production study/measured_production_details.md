# Measured production details: Nettspend "Winnie Harlow" (signal analysis of the user's WAV)

All numbers below were measured by me from the user's file (`1ba6c7d4-Nettspend_Archive_-_Nettspend_-_Winnie_Harlow.wav`: 44.1 kHz, 16-bit PCM, stereo, 137.230 s), using Python 3.11 with numpy 2.4, scipy 1.17, librosa 0.11, pyloudnorm, and my own implementations where noted. Each finding names its method. "Source" links point to the audio file that was measured. Stems came from **Demucs v4.1.0, model `htdemucs`** (shifts=1, overlap 0.25, CPU). They were used only for measurement and were deleted afterwards. Nothing here is a transcription: there are no note sequences, drum patterns, chord voicings or lyrics, only statistics and sound-design and mix facts.

**Timing grid used everywhere (measured):** 140.10 BPM (bar = 1.71305 s). Bar 1's downbeat is at **0.377 s**. The track is **80 bars** long. Method: a linear fit to all 80 clap onsets, each found at the 10 %-of-peak crossing of the drum stem's 1.5–5 kHz envelope. Residual 3.8 ms. This corrects ANALYSIS.md's "~0.31 s".

Confidence tags: **[H]** high (robust method, cross-checked), **[M]** medium (one method or stem-dependent), **[L]** low (confounded, treat as a hint).

### Takeaway
It's a very loud, flat master: **−9.2 LUFS integrated**, with a loudness range of only **1.1 LU**, a crest factor of about **10 dB**, and peaks at **0.0 dBFS sample / +0.4 dBTP**. The peaks aren't flat-topped (no runs of clipped samples). Instead, the sample density piles up just below about −1.5 dBFS, which fits heavy limiting or clipping *before* a lossy encode.

- Context: Spotify normalizes playback to −14 LUFS (ITU 1770) and turns louder masters down. ### Inferences
- The master sits about 4.8 LU above Spotify's reference, so Spotify plays it about 5 dB quieter. The loudness is a stylistic density choice (a flat, "wall" dynamic), not a playback-level advantage.
- The density hump plus the steep fall between −2 and −1 dBFS, *without* any flat-topped runs, suggests a limiter or clipper ceiling somewhere around −1.5 to −0.5 dBFS before lossy encoding. The decoder's overshoot then produced the few samples at 0 dBFS and the +0.4 dBTP inter-sample overs. Part of the −3 dB hump may simply be the arcsine amplitude distribution of the loud, sine-like 808, which piles samples up near its own peak. **[L]**
- **FL targets:** Fruity Limiter / Maximus on the master aiming for about −9 LUFS integrated, 9–10 dB crest, LRA ≤ 2 LU. Set the ceiling at about −1 dBFS (not 0) if the beat will be exported to MP3.

### Gaps
- I can't separate the original master's limiter settings from the codec's effect. Only an original lossless master would show the true ceiling and whether soft clipping was used.

## 3. 808

### Takeaway
The 808 fundamental lives in a narrow **~41–54 Hz band**. It is **moderately saturated**: the 3rd harmonic is only about 6 dB below the fundamental and the harmonics are audible to about the 5th–7th (about 250–350 Hz). It **sustains flat rather than decaying**, and it plays in long phrases (median about 1.2 bars of continuous sound) separated by short gaps. **Note changes are hard jumps. Glides are essentially absent** (at most 1 clear slide in 80 bars), which contradicts the generic "808 glides" convention noted in ANALYSIS.md.

- Sound design: a long, sustaining sine-type 808 (the equivalent of FL's sustain held with a long release) with soft, mostly odd-order saturation (tanh or soft clip). - Writing: notes connect into roughly 1–2-bar phrases, then short (about 1–2 sixteenth) breaths. Pitch moves by jumps, not portamento, so in FL leave "slide" notes and portamento off for this reference.

### Gaps
- Release shape and "rings into the next note" can't be measured cleanly. In the bass stem, the level dips about 8 dB for 20–80 ms after every kick. - In the full mix, harmonics above h5 overlap the melodic layer, so the exact count (7 or more) is uncertain.

## 5. Snare/clap

### Takeaway
The backbeat is a **dry, mono, mid-focused clap**: **3 bursts about 12 ms apart**, a spectral peak about **1.3 kHz** (−6 dB band 0.93–1.34 kHz), **almost no body below 600 Hz**, and **−20 dB within about 40–50 ms, −40 dB by about 75 ms**. There's **no measurable reverb tail**. It lands on beat 3 of **all 80 bars** with ±3.8 ms timing, so it is grid-quantized.

### Cited Findings
