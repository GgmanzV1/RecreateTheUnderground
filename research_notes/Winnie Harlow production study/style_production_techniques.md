Research date: 2026-09-29. Evidence labels used below:
- **[WIDE]**: the claim is repeated across 3 or more independent sources.
- **[SOME]**: 2 sources agree.
- **[SINGLE]**: only one source says it.
- **[VENDOR]**: the source sells a product it recommends (Output, Soundtrap, Native Instruments, Reason Studios). These are still useful, but they are not neutral.
- **[SNIPPET]**: the claim comes from a search-result summary; I could not open the page to confirm it.

## Q1. What tempos, feels and scales are typical, and how does this lane differ from mainstream trap, plugg/pluggnb, rage and jerk?

### Takeaway
Nearly every source puts this lane at **about 140–160 BPM, felt in half-time**. The snare or clap lands once per bar (beat 3 when counted at the full tempo). Keys are **minor** with short 2–4 chord loops. Plugg and pluggnb are the softer, slower (130–150), sparse-drum, jazzy-chord relatives. Rage is the harder, distorted, synth-lead version. Jerk (the 2020s 1c34/kashpaint strand that Nettspend is tied to) adds fidgety, staggered snares, mangled and pitched vocals, and blown-out bass.

### Cited Findings
- **Nettspend-type tempo and feel:** 140–160 BPM with half-time snare placement, so the beat feels slower than its BPM. Light 5–15% shuffle on hats only; kicks and snares stay on the grid. Snare on beat three rather than 2 and 4. Triplet grid for hat rolls and 808 slides. [SINGLE][VENDOR] — [Output blog, "Nettspend Type Beat Guide" (Mar 13, 2026)](https://output.com/blog/nettspend-type-beat)
- **Jerk-trap tempo:** 140–160 BPM, with a "sweet spot" around 150–152 BPM. 180–190 BPM is an occasional double-time, drill-crossover option. Snares on 2 and 4, layered with claps or rimshots. [SINGLE][VENDOR] — [Soundtrap blog, "Jerk Trap" (Aug 19, 2025)](https://blog.soundtrap.com/jerk-trap/). Note: this article covers "jerk/hoodtrap" in general and names no artists, so it may mix the 2020s underground jerk with the Detroit/Midwest hoodtrap "jerk" type beat.
- **Keys:** Soundtrap names B minor, G minor, A minor and F minor as typical jerk keys, with loops of 2–4 chords at most and some 7ths, extensions and passing tones — [Soundtrap](https://blog.soundtrap.com/jerk-trap/). Reason Studios says plugg favours minor keys and "two or three chords" — [Reason Studios, "How to Make Plugg Beats" (Jul 2, 2026)](https://www.reasonstudios.com/news/post/how-to-make-plugg-beats). **[SOME]** for minor keys with very short loops.
- **OsamaSon-type (an adjacent artist):** 140–160 BPM with a "frantic" feel. Snares and hats sit slightly off-grid. Dark atmospheric pads or eerie repeating melodies. [SINGLE, user-generated post, low reliability] — [Lemon8 post by prod.jayare](https://www.lemon8-app.com/@prod.jayare/7459541506190475822?region=us)
- **Plugg:**
  - 130–150 BPM, usually in half-time: "a 140 BPM beat can feel more like 70 BPM" while the hats keep the faster pulse.
  - Sparse drums; "busy hi-hat rolls belong in mainstream trap."
  - Soft pads, bells or electric pianos.
  - [VENDOR] — [Reason Studios](https://www.reasonstudios.com/news/post/how-to-make-plugg-beats)
- **Pluggnb:**
  - Worked example at 130 BPM.
  - Jazz-influenced extended chords (maj9 and min9 voicings with slight dissonance).
  - ROMpler-style keys and electric piano.
  - Clap anchored on beat 3.
  - [VENDOR] — [Native Instruments blog, Tim Cant (Mar 24, 2025)](https://blog.native-instruments.com/pluggnb-music/)
  - SampleFocus also gives 130–150 BPM for pluggnb [SNIPPET] — [SampleFocus blog](https://blog.samplefocus.com/blog/make-pluggnb-beats-samples/)
- **How the family fits together:**
  - Pluggnb is plugg plus R&B.
  - Rage "took plugg in a harder, more distorted direction."
  - Jerk "pulled its bright synths into nervous, glitchy drums."
  - [SNIPPET] — [SampleFocus blog](https://blog.samplefocus.com/blog/make-pluggnb-beats-samples/)
- **Rage traits:**
  - "Rage rap is all about distortion and pitch bending effects" — [BusyWorksBeats (Nov 2021)](https://busyworksbeats.com/blogs/music-production-tutorials/fl-studio-rage-melody-tutorial) [SINGLE, older]
  - Fan and genre wikis describe rage as distorted 808s, fast 1/16–1/32 hat rolls, and bright detuned supersaw or arpeggiated leads. Harmony is minimal but timbre is maximal, with risers and filter sweeps. [SNIPPET, low-reliability wikis] — [Melodigging, "Rage Rap"](https://www.melodigging.com/genre/rage-rap); [Hip-hop fandom wiki, "Rage"](https://hip-hop-music.fandom.com/wiki/Rage)
- **Jerk (2020s revival) character:**
  - The Face describes "fidgety rhythms, staggered snares and mangled vocals."
  - The style blends cloud rap, digicore, lowend and plugg.
  - 1c34 / Xaviersobased took percussion ideas from Milwaukee's lowend scene (per The FADER).
  - kashpaint developed the sound further; his "Patchmade" is credited with popularizing the microgenre.
  - [Wikipedia, "Jerk (music genre)", citing The Face, Pitchfork and The FADER](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerk_(music_genre))
- **Pitchfork's definition of jerk:** "pitched-up, layered vocals and bass that will make your speakers sound broken." The NYT called Nettspend's sound "post-post-rage." — [Wikipedia, "Nettspend", citing Pitchfork and NYT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nettspend)
- **Nettspend's sound in the press:** The FADER lists "distorted 808s," "loose piano riffs" and "glimmering synths," and notes most tracks run under two minutes. — [Wikipedia, "Nettspend", citing The FADER (Dec 2024)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nettspend)

### Inferences
- The two snare descriptions probably describe the same thing. At 150 BPM, "snare on beat 3" (Output) and "snares on 2 and 4" (Soundtrap) match if Soundtrap is counting in the half-time (~75 BPM) feel. A practical default is 145–160 BPM in FL with one clap or snare per bar on beat 3, fast hats on top.
- Practical differences between the neighbours:
  - Plugg/pluggnb: softer sounds, jazzier chords, sparse hats.
  - Rage: supersaw or arp leads plus heavily distorted 808s, with a steady grid.
  - Jerk: the snare and vocal rhythm is deliberately nervous and off-kilter, and vocals are pitched up.
  - The Nettspend lane borrows from all three.

### Gaps
- I found no source giving a key or BPM for specific Nettspend songs (e.g., "drankdrankdrank"). A Wikipedia page for that song returned 404.
- No reliable written breakdown of kashpaint or 1c34 drum patterns. Tutorials exist as videos only (see Q4).

## Q2. How are melodies made (synths, presets, samples, vocal chops) and processed?

### Takeaway
There are two main approaches:
- **Synth-led:** shimmering or "demonic/angelic" leads, bells and pads, often trance-influenced.
- **Sample-led:** pitched or chopped vocals, soul or alt-rock loops, lo-fi phrases.

Processing leans on reverb, delay, granular smearing, and tape or lo-fi colouring. Specific preset attributions for Nettspend are weak and mostly social-media claims.

### Cited Findings
- **Reklus1ve, a Nettspend producer:**
  - Aims for beats that feel "happy and evil."
  - Blends euphoric **trance**, the frayed "tread" style linked to the Working on Dying collective, and the witch-house band **Salem**.
  - Uses "demonic synths and angelic textures" for dissonant melodies. **zoot** ("drankdrankdrank") and **OK** are said to use a similar palette.
  - Reklus1ve and Votekk made "take you out" in seven minutes on the subway.
  - [SINGLE, primary press] — [i-D, Press-Reynolds & Maher (Sep 16, 2024)](https://i-d.co/article/nettspend-debut-album-profile-interview-2024/)
- **Recording:** Nettspend recorded early vocals on **BandLab** (mobile). — [i-D](https://i-d.co/article/nettspend-debut-album-profile-interview-2024/)
- **Sampling:** Nettspend sampled **Deftones** on "That One Song" (Jul 2024). This fits an alt-rock sampling habit. — [Wikipedia, "Nettspend", citing Stereogum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nettspend)
- **Evilgiane / Surf Gang** (producer of Nettspend and Xaviersobased's "40"):
  - Unusual sample choices ("you'll never find a sample like Giane does").
  - Tighter, more closed drums than typical NY drill; "woozy" cloud-rap-influenced melodies.
  - [SINGLE] — [The FADER, Vivian Medithi (Feb 27, 2024)](https://www.thefader.com/2024/02/27/evilgiane-heavensgate-vol-1-interview)
  - Other press describes "crystalline synthesizers," flipped pop hits, and indie or skate-video samples [SNIPPET] — [Stereogum](https://stereogum.com/2244988/evilgiane-surf-gang-are-changing-the-sound-of-hip-hop/interviews); [Alternative Press](https://www.altpress.com/evilgiane-surf-gang-interview/)
  - Search snippets say Evilgiane works in **Ableton**, not FL. [SNIPPET]
- **Sample sources and techniques (Nettspend-type):**
  - Pitched-down vocals, soul loops, lo-fi synth phrases.
  - Auto-slice to transients, pitch down for character, reverse sections.
  - [SINGLE][VENDOR] — [Output](https://output.com/blog/nettspend-type-beat)
  - Soundtrap similarly says to "slice, reverse, or pitch-shift" loops mid-track and to use bright synth stabs over moody pads — [Soundtrap](https://blog.soundtrap.com/jerk-trap/). **[SOME]** for slice, pitch and reverse.
- **Processing plugins named:**
  - Output Portal (granular delay; low density with long grains for washed-out textures; scale-locked pitch).
  - Output Thermal (distortion).
  - Output Movement (rhythmic modulation).
  - XLN **RC-20 Retro Color** (tape, distortion and "magnetic" wow/flutter modules).
  - [VENDOR, Output promoting its own products] — [Output](https://output.com/blog/nettspend-type-beat)
- **Plugg melody layering:** a bright bell layered with a warmer pad; pad, bell or electric piano as the main sound — [Reason Studios](https://www.reasonstudios.com/news/post/how-to-make-plugg-beats)
- **Pluggnb melody layering:**
  - Electric piano with a subtle delay.
  - A counter-melody synth set close to a sine, with ~19 ms glide.
  - Low-velocity strings.
  - [NI blog](https://blog.native-instruments.com/pluggnb-music/)
- **Presets:** Splice has a browsable "Rage" preset category (Serum, Vital and others) — [Splice, Rage presets](https://splice.com/sounds/genres/rage/presets). Searches also surfaced a Serum preset called "Stressed" linked to Nettspend and a "Paxor #ILOVEMUSIC" Serum 2 bank. Both came only from social or marketing snippets and are **unverified**.

### Inferences
- In FL terms, the "angelic" side is bells, choirs, pads or plucks drenched in reverb (Fruity Reeverb 2 or Valhalla-style), with ping-pong delay. The "demonic" side is a detuned supersaw or saw lead with distortion and pitch bends. Trance supersaws and witch-house darkness are the stated references, so a multi-voice detuned saw (Serum, Vital or FL's Sytrus/3xOsc) is a reasonable choice. This is my synthesis; no source gives unison or detune settings.
- Lo-fi, bitcrushed textures fit the "frayed" tread/Salem reference and RC-20-style tools. No source names a specific bitcrusher setting.

### Gaps
- No verified Omnisphere, Nexus, Serum or Vital preset names for any specific Nettspend beat.
- No written source for unison or detune values, formant tricks, or chorus settings for this lane.
- Video tutorials (OK, kashpaint) likely show presets, but I could not access them.

## Q3. How are 808s treated, and which FL Studio-specific methods apply?

### Takeaway
The consensus 808 is:
- **distorted or saturated** so it translates to phone speakers ("speakers sound broken" is the jerk ideal);
- **tuned to the root**;
- **gliding** between notes;
- sidechained to or complementary with the kick, with sub in mono.

In FL, glides use Mono + Porta in Channel Settings, or piano-roll slide/porta notes. Clipping is often the last stage of the 808 chain.

### Cited Findings
- **Nettspend-type 808 (Output):**
  - Choose 808s with upper harmonics.
  - Tune the root precisely to the key and sample.
  - Glide between notes.
  - Saturate for phone translation.
  - Keep sub below ~150 Hz in mono.
  - Sidechain to avoid mud.
  - Program the kick to *complement*, not copy, the 808 rhythm.
  - [VENDOR] — [Output](https://output.com/blog/nettspend-type-beat)
- **Jerk 808 (Soundtrap):**
  - "Subby, punchy, sometimes pitched up or gliding."
  - Sidechained to the kick.
  - Carve space around 60–80 Hz.
  - Optionally layer a higher-octave pluck or pad.
  - [VENDOR] — [Soundtrap](https://blog.soundtrap.com/jerk-trap/)
- **Plugg 808:** long release so it "rings out fully," with optional foldback distortion for grit — [Reason Studios](https://www.reasonstudios.com/news/post/how-to-make-plugg-beats)
- **Pluggnb 808:** octave-skipping lines and movement in thirds — [NI blog](https://blog.native-instruments.com/pluggnb-music/)
- **Consensus:** distorted, gliding 808s appear across Output, Soundtrap, the rage wikis, BusyWorks ("distortion and pitch bending") and FADER-via-Wikipedia ("distorted 808s"). **[WIDE]**
- **FL glide method A: Channel Settings.**
  - Enable **Mono** (one note at a time) and **Porta**.
  - The **Slide** knob sets portamento time.
  - **Gate** caps note length.
  - FL's portamento also glides cutoff, resonance and pan.
  - [Image-Line manual, Channel Settings – Misc](https://www.image-line.com/fl-studio-learning/fl-studio-online-manual/html/chansettings_misc.htm)
- **FL glide method B: Piano roll.**
  - **Slide notes** (S toggle): the overlap length sets the slide time.
  - **Portamento notes** (O toggle): a short glide into the flagged note.
  - Both **only work on native FL instruments**. For a VST, automate the channel pitch knob instead.
  - [Image-Line manual, Piano roll](https://www.image-line.com/fl-studio-learning/fl-studio-online-manual/html/pianoroll.htm)
- **Fruity Soft Clipper:** soft-knee compression near 0 dB with Threshold and Post-gain controls. It adds subtle saturation and "warmth," and distorts when pushed. The manual points to Fruity Limiter for more transparent protection. — [Image-Line manual, Fruity Soft Clipper](https://www.image-line.com/fl-studio-learning/fl-studio-online-manual/html/plugins/Fruity%20Soft%20Clipper.htm)
- **Fruity Limiter sidechain (kick ducks 808):**
  - Route the kick mixer track to the 808 track with "Sidechain to this track."
  - In Fruity Limiter's COMP tab, pick the kick as the SIDECHAIN source.
  - Set threshold, ratio, attack and release.
  - [Image-Line manual, Fruity Limiter](https://www.image-line.com/fl-studio-learning/fl-studio-online-manual/html/plugins/Fruity%20Limiter.htm)
- **Clipping at the end of the 808 chain:** third-party guides say a soft clipper there raises perceived loudness and eases the master limiter. One suggests a ~-3 dB clip threshold with oversampling on 808s. [SNIPPET] — [Unison, "Soft Clipping vs Hard Clipping"](https://unison.audio/soft-clipping-vs-hard-clipping/); [Support Hip Hop](https://supporthiphop.com/hip-hop-news/soft-clipping-vs-hard-clipping-in-hip-hop-production/)
- **Community tools:** Gumroad sells or gives away FL 808 distortion racks and a free "Better Clipper" FL plugin. These came up in search results only; their contents are unverified. — [Better Clipper (Gumroad)](https://prodbymaestro.gumroad.com/l/betterclipper)

### Inferences
- A plausible FL chain from the sources above (my synthesis, not a single tutorial's):
  1. 808 sample in FL's sampler (Channel: Mono + Porta, Slide ~ to taste).
  2. Tune to the key root.
  3. Saturation or distortion: Fruity Blood Overdrive, Fruity Fast Dist, or a third-party distortion.
  4. Parametric EQ 2 to tame mud and keep the sub.
  5. Fruity Limiter in sidechain from the kick.
  6. Fruity Soft Clipper last.
- For VST 808s (e.g., a Serum 808 patch), use the synth's own mono/legato glide, because piano-roll slide notes won't affect VSTs.

### Gaps
- I did not find a written tutorial that names Fruity Blood Overdrive, Transient Processor, Maximus or Fruity Delay 3 specifically for this lane. They're FL-native options, but not sourced here.
- There is no data on typical 808 decay or length or on specific 808 samples used by Nettspend's producers.

## Q4. Hi-hat programming, roll habits and drum sources

### Takeaway
Hats run fast on a 1/16 base with **triplet (1/8T, 1/16T) and 1/32 rolls**, velocity ramps, light swing and occasional pitch-shifted layers. Kicks are sparse and syncopated against the 808. There is one main clap or snare per bar in half-time, plus ghost or staggered snares for the jerk "fidget." Kits are mostly commercial or free "type" packs. I found no verifiable producer-specific kit for Nettspend's team.

### Cited Findings
- **Nettspend-type hats and percussion:**
  - Velocity-ramped rolls with triplet bursts, and open-hat accents.
  - 5–15% swing on hats; straight kicks and snares.
  - Percussion: shakers, rim clicks, foley and vocal-chop accents.
  - The snare is punchy with a short reverb, layered with a clap or rim.
  - [VENDOR] — [Output](https://output.com/blog/nettspend-type-beat)
- **Jerk hats and snares:**
  - Triplet rolls or stuttered 1/16s, with swung hats over rigid kicks.
  - Ghost-note snares for syncopation.
  - Nudge claps a few ms off-grid.
  - Short, punchy kicks that leave space for vocals.
  - [VENDOR] — [Soundtrap](https://blog.soundtrap.com/jerk-trap/)
- **"Staggered snares"** is a defining trait of jerk. — [Wikipedia, "Jerk (music genre)", citing The Face](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerk_(music_genre))
- **Evilgiane-style drums:** "sporadic 808s that hit on the downbeat and snares that slice through seemingly-random hi-hat patterns" [SNIPPET] — [Stereogum](https://stereogum.com/2244988/evilgiane-surf-gang-are-changing-the-sound-of-hip-hop/interviews). Tighter, closed drums — [The FADER](https://www.thefader.com/2024/02/27/evilgiane-heavensgate-vol-1-interview)
- **General roll technique:**
  - Rolls at 1/16, 1/32 and even 1/64.
  - For triplets, set the piano-roll grid to 1/8T or 1/16T.
  - Velocity tiers of ~100 for main hits, ~60 for ghosts, ~30 for fillers.
  - Layer hats pitched ±2 semitones, or modulate hat pitch ±3 semitones over 4 bars.
  - [Unison, "Hi-Hat Rolls" (2024)](https://unison.audio/hi-hat-rolls/) [SINGLE for the pitch numbers]
- **Pluggnb drums:**
  - Open hats on 8ths with 16th and 32nd rolls.
  - Snare rolls at 16th and 32nd.
  - Shaker every other quarter note.
  - Clap on beat 3.
  - [NI blog](https://blog.native-instruments.com/pluggnb-music/)
- **Rage hats:** tight, fast trap hats with 1/16–1/32 rolls, and sparse but impactful kicks. [SNIPPET, low-reliability wiki] — [Melodigging](https://www.melodigging.com/genre/rage-rap)
- **Drum sources surfaced (legit storefronts or free sites only):**
  - Splice "OPIUM – Rage Type Beats" (Origin Sound) — [Splice](https://splice.com/sounds/packs/origin-sound/opium-rage-type-beats/samples)
  - Soundtrap "illegal" rage pack — [Soundtrap](https://www.soundtrap.com/content/originals/rage-trap-sample-pack)
  - LANDR "Pierre Bourne"-style drum kit collection — [LANDR](https://samples.landr.com/collections/royalty-free-pierre-bourne-drum-kit)
  - SampleFocus free "Jerk Beat Drum Kit" and jerk loops — [SampleFocus](https://samplefocus.com/collections/jerk-beat-drum-kit)
  - evilgiane kits and beats on Traktrain — [Traktrain](https://traktrain.com/evilgiane)
- **Video tutorials that exist (content not verified; YouTube was blocked to the fetcher):**
  - [How OK Makes Beats For NETTSPEND](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GKjS1n4_hOs)
  - [How To Make BEATS For NETTSPEND Like OK (FL Studio)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UZs6bGUo5Jk)
  - [HOW TO Make Beats For NETTSPEND](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bgYm8a2NUkY)
  - [How To Make JERK Beats Like KASHPAINT For XAVIERSOBASED (FL Studio)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_ZL-ZIRsjBo)
  - [How Kashpaint Makes Jerk Beats for XavierSoBased & SiyahXO (FL)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CcuriKzlf34)

  - Morabeats Jerk/Hoodtrap FL 2024 tutorial page (video plus templates) — [Morabeats (Nov 2024)](https://morabeats.com/en/2024/11/17/tutorial-como-hacer-jerk-hoodtrap-beat/)

### Inferences
- A starting FL pattern (my synthesis):
  - 150 BPM, 1/16 hats with velocity variation.
  - A 1/16T or 1/32 roll at the end of every 1–2 beats.
  - One or two hats pitched up or down a few semitones in the roll, using note fine-pitch or a pitch-shifted duplicate channel.
  - Clap on beat 3.
  - For jerk flavour, add 1–2 extra ghost snares on off-16ths, and shift a clap a few ms late.
- Plugg and pluggnb want *fewer* rolls. If the beat starts sounding like mainstream trap, cut the roll density (per Reason Studios).

## Q5. Arrangement habits (loop-based structure, mute tricks)

### Takeaway
Songs are short (often under 2 minutes). Beats are made fast and built on short loops. Variation comes from **mutes and drops** (808 out, drums out), **sample flips**, and filter sweeps rather than new sections.

### Cited Findings
- **Short songs:** most Nettspend tracks are under two minutes. — [Wikipedia, "Nettspend", citing The FADER](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nettspend)
- **Speed:** beats are sometimes made in minutes ("take you out" in seven minutes on a train), and vocals were recorded in ~10 minutes. — [i-D](https://i-d.co/article/nettspend-debut-album-profile-interview-2024/)
- **Arrangement template (Output):**
  - Intro: a filtered sample or beat.
  - Verses: pull back drums or melody.
  - Hook: everything in, plus ear candy.
  - Switches: mute 808 sections, drop drums, flip the sample.
  - Filter sweeps and volume automation mark transitions.
  - [VENDOR] — [Output](https://output.com/blog/nettspend-type-beat)
- **Jerk variation:** slice, reverse or pitch the loop mid-track, automate a subtle filter sweep, and keep chord loops at 2–4 chords. — [Soundtrap](https://blog.soundtrap.com/jerk-trap/). **[SOME]** with Output.
- **Rage builds:** intensity comes from looping motifs, risers and filter sweeps. [SNIPPET] — [Melodigging](https://www.melodigging.com/genre/rage-rap)
- **Gross Beat for FL glitch transitions:**
  - Real-time time and volume manipulation over a 2-bar buffer.
  - 36 time and 36 volume envelope slots for half-speed, reverse, tape-stop, stutter, repeat and gating.
  - Slots can be triggered live or automated.
  - [Image-Line manual, Gross Beat](https://www.image-line.com/fl-studio-learning/fl-studio-online-manual/html/plugins/Gross%20Beat.htm)

### Inferences
- In FL, build one 4- or 8-bar pattern. Then, in the Playlist, arrange copies with the 808 or drum clips muted for 1–2 bars before hooks. Automate Gross Beat slots (half-speed or stutter) on the last beat before a switch. This is my synthesis; no source prescribes Gross Beat for this specific lane.

### Gaps
- There is no source-based bar-by-bar structure for a real Nettspend track.

## Q6. Mix and master habits (loudness, clipping, dark vs bright, sidechain)

### Takeaway
The lane aims **loud and dense**: trap and rap masters commonly land around **-8 to -10 LUFS integrated**. Low end is **saturated or clipped**, with a **soft clipper before the final limiter**, and kick and 808 separated by sidechain. Jerk's stated ideal is bass that makes speakers "sound broken." I found no source that documents a deliberately "dark top end" for this lane.

### Cited Findings
- **Loudness targets:** 808-heavy trap is commonly mastered at -8 to -10 LUFS integrated, with -1.0 dBTP true peak (or -2.0 dBTP when very loud) and a 6–9 dB crest factor. — [BeatsToRapOn, "Rap Mastering Settings 2025" (Oct 1, 2025)](https://beatstorapon.com/blog/rap-mastering-settings-2025-professional-targets-presets-and-platform-delivery-for-rap-trap-rb/) [SINGLE]
- **"Hyped" master chain:** M/S EQ (mono lows) → exciter/saturator → multiband compression → **soft clipper** → limiter. The clipper shaves peaks so the limiter works less hard. — [BeatsToRapOn](https://beatstorapon.com/blog/rap-mastering-settings-2025-professional-targets-presets-and-platform-delivery-for-rap-trap-rb/)
- **Clipper before the limiter:** also repeated in clipping guides, which name StandardCLIP and KClip as common third-party clippers. [SNIPPET] — [Unison](https://unison.audio/soft-clipping-vs-hard-clipping/); [Support Hip Hop](https://supporthiphop.com/hip-hop-news/soft-clipping-vs-hard-clipping-in-hip-hop-production/). **[SOME–WIDE]** for "soft clip then limit."
- **Plugg mixing:**
  - A soft clipper on the mix bus for warmth.
  - High-pass the melodies so the 808 owns the lows.
  - Use send reverb and delay.
  - A light overall touch.
  - [Reason Studios](https://www.reasonstudios.com/news/post/how-to-make-plugg-beats)
- **Sidechain kick→808:** recommended by [Output](https://output.com/blog/nettspend-type-beat) and [Soundtrap](https://blog.soundtrap.com/jerk-trap/) **[SOME]**. For the FL implementation, see the [Fruity Limiter manual](https://www.image-line.com/fl-studio-learning/fl-studio-online-manual/html/plugins/Fruity%20Limiter.htm).
- **Other mix moves:**
  - Saturate 808 harmonics for phone speakers; sub in mono below ~150 Hz — [Output](https://output.com/blog/nettspend-type-beat)
  - Short reverbs on snares and claps; tempo-synced delays — [Soundtrap](https://blog.soundtrap.com/jerk-trap/)
- **Jerk's "broken speakers" bass aesthetic:** — [Wikipedia citing Pitchfork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nettspend)
- **Fruity Soft Clipper as the FL-native soft clipper:** Threshold and Post-gain; saturates when pushed. — [Image-Line manual](https://www.image-line.com/fl-studio-learning/fl-studio-online-manual/html/plugins/Fruity%20Soft%20Clipper.htm)
- **Pluggnb mastering (for contrast):** iZotope Ozone Assistant, switched to an "RnB/Soul" profile for a softer, fuller master. This is gentler than rage or jerk. — [NI blog](https://blog.native-instruments.com/pluggnb-music/)

### Inferences
- FL master chain consistent with these sources:
  1. Parametric EQ 2 (low cut below ~25–30 Hz; mono low end with Stereo Shaper if needed).
  2. Optional saturation.
  3. Fruity Soft Clipper (or a third-party clipper), set so it only shaves peaks.
  4. Fruity Limiter or Maximus set to about -1 dBTP.
  5. Check that the integrated level sits roughly in the -8 to -10 LUFS region.
- Loudness is pursued, but the low end is *meant* to crunch. So distortion on the 808 bus is a stylistic choice, not an error.
- The "dark top end" idea may come from lo-fi and tape processing (RC-20) and from muffled, washed sample textures, not from a documented mastering EQ habit. Treat it as a hypothesis to check by ear against references.
