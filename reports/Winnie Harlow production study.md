# Decoding ok's "Winnie Harlow" for FL Studio

"Winnie Harlow" is an official Nettspend release produced by **ok** (wegonebeok, credited as Minnix). It is not a leak: it is track 16, a bonus found only on the $8 webstore "Winnie Version" of *Bad Ass F\*cking Kid*, and the SoundCloud and Audiomack "archive" copies are fan re-uploads posted on December 12 and 14, 2024. The "KwickedOnAllPlats" credit is a TikTok bio string that aggregators misread as a producer name. The only BPM/key listing, Chordify's "136 BPM, G♯ minor", is known only from a search snippet; it has the key right and the tempo slightly wrong. Measured from your copy, the song is **140.10 BPM half-time in G♯ natural minor, tuned about +15 cents sharp, 80 bars long**. It rests on **one 4-bar beat loop in which no layer ever drops out**, and every change you hear as arrangement comes from the vocal. The sound targets are precise, and several contradict the genre playbook. The **808 sustains, is moderately saturated (3rd harmonic about 6 dB below the fundamental) and jumps between notes rather than gliding**. A **separate dark, pitch-swept kick** punches over it. A **dry three-burst clap hits beat 3 of every bar**. **8th-note hats roll in 51 of 80 bars**, and a **wide, soft-attack chordal layer sits about 13 LU under the beat**. The master is **−9.2 LUFS with 1.1 LU of loudness range and a 10 dB crest factor**. The gaps matter too. Neither ok nor Nettspend has talked about this song. No one on Nettspend's production team has published a plugin, gear or kit list, so every "ok uses X" claim comes from fan remakers. And your file is a ~128 kbps-class lossy decode, so anything above about 15 kHz is missing.

*How to read the labels.* **[High]** means an official or primary source, several agreeing sources, or a robust measurement that was cross-checked. **[Medium]** means one reasonable secondary source, a single measurement method, or a number that depends on stem separation. **[Low]** means a fan claim, a search snippet, or a confounded measurement. *Single-source*, *vendor* (the source sells the tools it recommends), *snippet-only* and *remaker* (a fan recreation, not the original producer) flag weaker evidence. The measurements come from signal analysis of your WAV (BS.1770 loudness, pYIN/YIN pitch tracking, Welch spectra, with Demucs htdemucs stems used only for measurement). Fan re-uploads of the paid track, stem-separated fan "instrumentals" and leaked kits are described below but deliberately not linked.

## The lane's playbook: half-time grids, saturated 808s, clip-then-limit masters

**Tempo and feel are the most consistent claims about this lane.** Sources put Nettspend-type, jerk and plugg beats at **roughly 140–160 BPM felt in half-time**, with one snare or clap per bar on beat 3. In Reason Studios' phrase, "a 140 BPM beat can feel more like 70 BPM" while the hats keep the fast pulse ([Output](https://output.com/blog/nettspend-type-beat); [Soundtrap](https://blog.soundtrap.com/jerk-trap/); [Reason Studios](https://www.reasonstudios.com/news/post/how-to-make-plugg-beats)) **[three sources agree, all vendors]**. Keys are minor, with loops of two to four chords **[two vendor sources]**. The neighbouring styles differ in attitude. Pluggnb is plugg plus jazzier R&B chords. Rage takes plugg "in a harder, more distorted direction" with supersaw or arpeggiated leads. Jerk adds "fidgety rhythms, staggered snares and mangled vocals" ([Wikipedia: Jerk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerk_%28music_genre%29); [SampleFocus](https://blog.samplefocus.com/blog/make-pluggnb-beats-samples/), snippet-only). Speed is part of the workflow: Reklus1ve and Votekk made "take you out" in about seven minutes on the C train, and Nettspend recorded his early vocals on BandLab ([i-D](https://i-d.co/article/nettspend-debut-album-profile-interview-2024/)).

**Image-Line's manual documents the FL Studio mechanics behind the standard moves.** For the gliding 808 the lane is known for, turn on **Mono** and **Porta** in the channel's Misc settings and set the glide time with the **Slide** knob ([Image-Line: Channel Settings](https://www.image-line.com/fl-studio-learning/fl-studio-online-manual/html/chansettings_misc.htm)). The alternative is to flag piano-roll **slide notes**, where the overlap sets the glide time, or **portamento notes**. Both work only on native FL instruments, so a VST 808 needs its own legato glide or pitch automation ([Image-Line: Piano roll](https://www.image-line.com/fl-studio-learning/fl-studio-online-manual/html/pianoroll.htm)). Kick-to-808 ducking works by routing the kick's mixer track into the 808's Fruity Limiter as a sidechain source on the COMP tab ([Image-Line: Fruity Limiter](https://www.image-line.com/fl-studio-learning/fl-studio-online-manual/html/plugins/Fruity%20Limiter.htm)). **Fruity Soft Clipper** shaves peaks with a soft knee and saturates when pushed ([Image-Line: Soft Clipper](https://www.image-line.com/fl-studio-learning/fl-studio-online-manual/html/plugins/Fruity%20Soft%20Clipper.htm)). Hat rolls come from snapping the piano roll to a 32nd or triplet grid; in FL's snap menu, "1/2 step" gives 32nds and "1/6 beat" gives 16th triplets. One guide suggests velocity tiers of about 100 for main hits, 60 for ghosts and 30 for fillers ([Unison](https://unison.audio/hi-hat-rolls/)) **[single-source]**. Gross Beat's time and volume envelopes supply half-speed, stutter and tape-stop switch-ups ([Image-Line: Gross Beat](https://www.image-line.com/fl-studio-learning/fl-studio-online-manual/html/plugins/Gross%20Beat.htm)). On the master, the recurring advice is a soft clipper in front of the final limiter, landing at **−8 to −10 LUFS integrated and −1 dBTP** with a **6–9 dB crest factor** ([BeatsToRapOn](https://beatstorapon.com/blog/rap-mastering-settings-2025-professional-targets-presets-and-platform-delivery-for-rap-trap-rb/)) **[single-source for the numbers; "clip then limit" is repeated in other guides]**.

### Lock the grid first: 140.10 BPM, bar 1 at 0.377 s, tuning +15 cents

**The grid is exact enough to A/B against.** A straight-line fit to all 80 clap onsets gives **140.101 BPM** (one bar = 1.71305 s), with bar 1's downbeat at **0.377 s**, and the file holds exactly 80 bars ([measured, your WAV](file:///root/.claude/uploads/9a4ea5ec-47ae-579f-b007-9ae087a0d50b/1ba6c7d4-Nettspend_Archive_-_Nettspend_-_Winnie_Harlow.wav)) **[High]**. The first 0.37 s is silence and the music enters on the downbeat, so trimming the leading silence lines the file up with FL's bar 1. Turn off time-stretching on the clip so it plays at its real speed. For A/B work, set the project tempo to **140.10, not 140**. At 140.00 each bar is 1.24 ms longer, and that adds up to about **0.1 s, nearly a full 16th note, by bar 80** (my calculation from the fitted bar length). For your own beat, 140 is fine.

**Everything is tuned about 15 cents sharp.** The 808's median offset from A440 equal temperament is **+16.2 cents**. 97 % of the melodic partials sit within 15 cents of a +15-cent grid. The pitch-corrected vocal lands within ±10 cents of that shifted grid 86 % of the time, against only 25 % of the time for plain A440 ([measured, your WAV](file:///root/.claude/uploads/9a4ea5ec-47ae-579f-b007-9ae087a0d50b/1ba6c7d4-Nettspend_Archive_-_Nettspend_-_Winnie_Harlow.wav)) **[High]**. In FL, tune your 808, keys and pads up 15 cents (channel or synth fine-tune), or set a tuner or Auto-Tune reference to **A = 443.8 Hz**. One inference narrows down where the offset comes from. A speed-up large enough to raise pitch 15 cents would also raise tempo 0.87 %, turning a 140.00 BPM session into about 141.2 BPM, yet the file measures 140.10. Unless ok's session ran at an odd 138.9 BPM, the sharpness is **built into the sounds or the session's tuning, not caused by a speed change somewhere in the upload chain** **[Medium; my inference]**. The key is G♯ natural minor. The 808's fundamentals span about 41.5–53.5 Hz, roughly E1 to G♯1 at this tuning, which fits the Chordify chord set.

### The master is loud through sustained low end, not squashed peaks

**The whole song stays within a 2 dB loudness window.** Integrated loudness is **−9.2 LUFS** (two independent BS.1770 implementations agree within 0.04 LU). Loudness range is **1.1 LU**, and the crest factor is **10 dB**. Every one of the 79 full bars peaks within 0.77 dB of full scale ([measured, your WAV](file:///root/.claude/uploads/9a4ea5ec-47ae-579f-b007-9ae087a0d50b/1ba6c7d4-Nettspend_Archive_-_Nettspend_-_Winnie_Harlow.wav)) **[High]**.

| Metric | Your copy | FL target |
|---|---|---|
| Integrated loudness | −9.2 LUFS | about −9 LUFS |
| Short-term max / median | −8.5 / −9.2 LUFS | short-term within ~1 LU all song |
| Momentary max | −5.6 LUFS | (not a target) |
| Loudness range (EBU Tech 3342) | 1.1 LU | ≤ 2 LU |
| Crest factor (RMS −10.05 dBFS) | 10.05 dB; PLR 9.6 dB | 9–10 dB |
| Sample / true peak | 0.0 dBFS / +0.4 dBTP (codec overshoot) | −1 dBTP ceiling (−2 dBTP for streaming) |
| Flat-topped clipping | none (longest run at the maximum: 2 samples) | (not a target) |

The 10 dB crest is the tell. The loudness comes from **an 808 that sustains under almost the whole song**, with 37 % of all energy between 20 and 60 Hz, not from grinding the peaks down. That crest is slightly above the 6–9 dB range the single mastering guide gives. Sample levels pile up just below −1.5 dBFS and fall away steeply above it. That pattern fits a limiter or clipper ceiling around −1.5 to −0.5 dBFS applied **before** a lossy encode, with the decoder then overshooting to 0 dBFS **[Low; interpretation]**. A lossy encode would also blur any flat-topped clipping, so a clipper in the original chain can't be ruled out. In FL, **Fruity Soft Clipper into Fruity Limiter or Maximus** gets you to this target. Spotify normalizes playback to −14 LUFS, turns louder masters down, and asks those louder masters to keep true peak below −2 dBTP ([Spotify for Artists](https://support.spotify.com/us/artists/article/loudness-normalization/)). This master therefore plays about 5 dB quieter there. Its loudness is a choice about density, not a playback-level advantage.

**Match the tonal balance by octave, and stop at 15 kHz.** Your file is a decoded lossy stream with a brick wall at about **16 kHz**: the median per-frame cutoff is 15.8 kHz, and the level is 72 dB down by 17 kHz. ### A sustaining 808 that jumps, under a separate kick that punches through it

**The 808 is a long, moderately saturated sine that changes pitch by jumping.** Its fundamental stays in a narrow **~41–54 Hz** band. Relative to the fundamental, the harmonics measure **h2 −15, h3 −6, h4 −16 and h5 −28 dB**. Odd harmonics are about 6 dB stronger than even ones, and audible content reaches roughly the 5th–7th harmonic (250–350 Hz) ([measured, your WAV](file:///root/.claude/uploads/9a4ea5ec-47ae-579f-b007-9ae087a0d50b/1ba6c7d4-Nettspend_Archive_-_Nettspend_-_Winnie_Harlow.wav)) **[High for h2–h5]**. The 3rd harmonic, only 6 dB down and landing around 125–160 Hz, is what makes this 808 audible on phone speakers, while the 5th is already 28 dB down. That profile points to soft, symmetric saturation, not a hard-clipped rage 808. The level **doesn't decay** during notes (median slope +1.2 dB/s). The 808 plays in phrases with a median length of about **1.2 bars**, separated by breaths of about **190 ms**, and sounds 77 % of the time **[Medium]**. There are **61 pitch changes across the 80 bars (about six per eight bars)**. Of the 22 that happen within continuous sound, 15 are instant jumps, 6 take a single cycle and **only one is an audible slide** **[Medium-High]**.

In FL, start from a sustaining 808, either a sample with a sustain loop or a synth 808 with full sustain, and for this reference turn **Mono on and Porta off**. Add saturation with Fruity Soft Clipper pushed into distortion, or with a gentle symmetric curve in Fruity WaveShaper, and watch an analyzer until the 3rd harmonic sits about 6 dB and the 2nd about 15 dB below the fundamental. To copy the stereo split, send the 808 to two mixer tracks: low-pass one below 80 Hz and keep it mono, then high-pass the other and widen or chorus it.

**The kick is a separate, dark, pitch-swept sub kick.** There are about **2.4 kicks per bar** (192 in total). 93 % fall within ±15 ms of the 16th grid, and none land on beat 3. They appear to come from a single sample, since every kick's low-end peak sits at 59.2 Hz ([measured, your WAV](file:///root/.claude/uploads/9a4ea5ec-47ae-579f-b007-9ae087a0d50b/1ba6c7d4-Nettspend_Archive_-_Nettspend_-_Winnie_Harlow.wav)) **[Medium-High]**. Each kick sweeps from about **230–270 Hz down to ~55 Hz within 80 ms**, peaks at 50–70 Hz, lasts about **130–150 ms** and has almost no click (1 kHz is about 56 dB below the peak). Of the 130 kicks where the 808's pitch could be tracked before and after, 85 land on an 808 that is already sustaining with no pitch change and 45 coincide with a pitch change. ### A dry beat-3 clap and 8th-note hats that roll in two bars out of three

**The backbeat is a dry, mid-focused clap, quantized hard.** It lands on beat 3 of **all 80 bars** (±3.8 ms) and shows the classic clap signature of **three bursts about 12 ms apart**. In FL, use one clap sample high-passed at 500–600 Hz, with a presence boost at 1–1.3 kHz and a smaller one near 2.5 kHz. Give it no reverb send and keep it centred.

**The hats sit on an 8th-note base and roll often.** There are about 11.5 hat hits per bar (range 6–20), and 8th-note spacing is the most common gap between hits. **51 of 80 bars contain at least one roll** (75 rolls in all), and 80 % of rolls are **3–5 hits** long. ## Conclusion

"Winnie Harlow" is defined by ratios, not tricks. The genre playbook, and the earlier analysis that leaned on it, reaches for glides, pitched rolls, sidechain pumping, mute drops and lo-fi wobble. The measured song uses none of them. Instead it holds a handful of relationships steady for 80 bars: a kick peak 8–10 dB over a flat 808 sustain, a 3rd harmonic 6 dB under the 808's fundamental, mono below 80 Hz and width above it, a clap 17 dB clear of its surroundings, and a melody 13 LU under the beat. A remake that hits those ratios at −9 LUFS with a 10 dB crest will read as this song even with different notes. That also makes it the cleanest way to learn from the track without copying its parts. Write your own 4-bar loop in G♯ minor at 140, then check it against these numbers rather than against a transcription. The same finding reframes ok's reputation for "angelic" production. On this track the angelic quality is an engineering result, a quiet, wide, soft-edged layer over a mono floor, more than it is a choice of preset.

The open questions are few and cheap to close. Buying the $8 Winnie Version would settle the 2:49-versus-2:17 length question and show whether your copy is an edit. It would also give you a first-generation file for re-measuring the ceiling and top end, though it will still be an MP3. Watching ok's archived IG-live cookups is the only way to learn what he actually runs in FL, because every plugin name in circulation comes from remakers and the ok-branded kits for sale have no provenance. Until then, treat the +15-cent tuning as part of the sounds themselves and tune your session to it. That one setting decides whether your A/B comparisons sound right.
